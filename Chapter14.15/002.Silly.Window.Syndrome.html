Technical Analysis: SWS Avoidance

Sender-Side Strategy: At t=0.053 (Packet 5), the sender holds back. Even though it has more data, it waits until t=5.066 to transmit. This avoids sending tiny segments that would waste overhead.

The Probe Mechanism: Packet 9 shows a ZeroWindowProbe. The sender forces the receiver to respond with an ACK to see if the window has finally opened up.

Receiver-Side Strategy: Seen at Packet 14, the receiver continues to advertise a Zero Window even after data is consumed. It waits until it can offer a "respectable" window size (1 MSS or half the buffer) before letting the floodgates open.

This dance prevents "Silly Window Syndrome"—a state where TCP moves data in tiny 1-byte chunks, effectively killing throughput.
