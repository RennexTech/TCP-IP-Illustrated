TCP CUBIC

The default logic for Linux and modern high-speed networks.

Concave Growth

After a loss, CUBIC grows quickly at first but slows down as it approaches its previous high speed.

The Plateau

Near the old limit, it levels off. This makes it very stable and "friendly" to other users on the network.

Convex Probe

If no loss happens at the plateau, CUBIC starts searching for even more speed, growing faster and faster.

Why a Cubic Function?

Classic TCP uses a straight line. CUBIC uses a "S-Curve". This shape is perfect because it spend most of its time near the estimated ideal speed, reducing the "sawtooth" jitter found in older versions.

It scales effortlessly. Whether you are on a 10 Mbps home link or a 100 Gbps data center pipe, CUBIC adapts its growth automatically.

The Cruise Ship Analogy

If Classic TCP is like a car constantly hitting the gas and then the brakes, CUBIC is like a massive Cruise Ship. It takes a bit to get up to speed, but once it finds the right velocity, it stays there with incredible stability, only adjusting its power when it is absolutely sure it can go faster.
